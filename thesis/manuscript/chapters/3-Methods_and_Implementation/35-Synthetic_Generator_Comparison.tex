\section{Comparing Synthetic-Image Generators}\label{sec:generator_comparison}

The synthetic imagery of \Cref{sec:synthetic_data_supplementation} originated from a single generative model that was selected through a visual comparison with a few alternatives early on. This choice is critical. If a cheaper or free model would have served just as well, or if apparent realism does not translate into detection accuracy, then the conclusions of the synthetic-data experiments would change. The second possibility is not hypothetical. Earlier work by the present author found that generated image context conferred no downstream advantage over random noise on a small-object car-detection task \cite{hedrichGeneratingSyntheticDatasets2026}. This section describes the controlled comparison built to test this finding: what was compared, how it was made fair, and how the generators were judged.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Scoped Research Question}\label{sec:generator_comparison_motivation}

The work's question about synthetic data is whether current image-generation models can produce training data useful for wildlife detection, particularly for long-tail species. The term \enquote{Useful} is meaningful only in relation to other options, and a study using a single model cannot distinguish a property of synthetic data from a property of a specific vendor's model. The scoped, secondary question is therefore:

\begin{quote}
On a purposively chosen subset of classes, how do synthetic images from different generators (proprietary API services and locally-run open-weight models) compare, both under a structured qualitative rubric and under downstream detection accuracy measured on real photographs, and does the resulting ranking justify the production choice?
\end{quote}

Most of the synthetic-image budget had already been consumed by the production run, so regenerating the full dataset per candidate was never an option. Instead, the comparison runs on a reduced class set with a modest number of images per class.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Experimental Design}\label{sec:generator_comparison_design}

The design is a single-factor experiment with one secondary factor that was deliberately introduced. The class set, number of images per class, annotation procedure, detector, hyperparameters, augmentation policy, and real test set are fixed. Therefore, any difference in outcome belongs to the generator.

\subsection{Class-Subset Selection}\label{sec:generator_comparison_classes}

The subset must expose three phenomena: long-tail species under-represented in the generators' pretraining data, classes with enough real test images to separate a difference from noise, and near-identical species that test fine-grained markers. The rarest species also have the fewest real test images, so the subset covers all three as a set rather than in every class.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Prompt Regimes}\label{sec:generator_comparison_prompts}

The production prompts are about $1{,}300$ words long, but the SDXL-family text encoder stops reading after $77$ tokens \cite{radfordLearningTransferableVisual2021}. Therefore, the same text does not provide both models with the same information. Measuring a gap in this way confounds how well a model generates with how much it reads.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{The three prompt regimes and what each is for.}
\label{tab:generator-prompt-regimes}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}llX@{}}
\toprule
Regime & Budget & Purpose \\
\midrule
\texttt{full} & $\approx 1{,}300$ words & Each API model at its maximum, as in production \\
\texttt{compressed} & $\leq 75$ tokens & An \emph{identical} prompt every model can read \\
\texttt{maxlen} & $75/256/512$ tokens & Each local model at its encoder's capacity \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The \texttt{compressed} prompt is dense and ordered by importance since an encoder weighs early tokens most heavily and discards the rest. A trimming rule first drops habitat, then pose, and then reduces diagnostic features to one. This ensures that the species name and at least one marker survive the longest.

The \texttt{maxlen} regime is built by accumulation rather than truncation. It grows a short template until the next description sentence exceeds the budget. The per-image pose and environment carry over from the production image list so that the prompts maintain real per-image variation. Measured lengths fall below the nominal ceilings (\Cref{sec:appendix_prompt_token_budgets}). \Cref{fig:prompt-regime-example} illustrates the extent of the difference for one class. \Cref{sec:appendix_prompt_regime_examples} reproduces all three prompts for that image in full.

Two asymmetries are disclosed rather than smoothed over. The \texttt{full} regime is not byte-identical across vendors since one API caps prompts at $32{,}000$ characters. Consequently, species with long articles had their descriptions trimmed. Additionally, negative prompts could not be applied uniformly because the FLUX-family pipeline accepts none, resulting in one cell lacking a control that the others have.

Reproducibility varies by tier. Local models are deterministic with per-image seeds from a fixed class ordering. The API services do not expose a seed. Therefore, the archived images and request parameters are the reproducible artifacts. Within the local tier, the two short regimes index seeds differently. Therefore, the same slot in each regime is not generated from the same seed. This adds noise to any comparison between the two regimes.

This is largely why an API service was the practical production choice, regardless of output quality. \Cref{tab:generator-api-cost} prices the four API cells. The production generator returns a second image in about a third of its responses after reviewing its own output. \subsection{Training Protocol}\label{sec:generator_comparison_training}

Every cell was annotated by the detector \cite{beeryEfficientPipelineCamera2019} and procedure of \Cref{sec:synthetic_annotation}. One deviation qualifies every downstream number in \Cref{sec:results_synthetic_data_practicability}: the human review stages were never executed here, so all cells use the detector's own boxes. While the ranking is unlikely to be an annotation artifact since every cell was annotated identically, the absolute figures are not final. Single-animal yield ranged from $93.6\%$ to $98.8\%$ across local cells, which is a quality signal itself.

\textit{YOLO26n} \cite{UltralyticsYOLO262025} was fixed as the detector for every cell. The available alternative, \textit{YOLOv5s} \cite{ultralyticsComprehensiveGuideUltralytics}, is roughly the size of the detector that already ships, whereas \textit{YOLO26n} is the architecture class this work targets for embedded deployment, so a result on it stays load-bearing for the main study.

Model selection and early stopping use a per-class, stratified fifth of each cell's synthetic images. These images are drawn with a seed that is independent of the training seed. This ensures that the split is identical across a cell's repeated runs. The real test set is scored once per run on the best checkpoint.

Because a single run on a dataset this small produces noisy results, each cell was trained with several seeds and averaged. Three seeds were used for each API cell and two for each local cell, which is less than the three recommended by the protocol.

\subsection{Evaluation Methodology}\label{sec:generator_comparison_evaluation}

The judgment this sub-study replaces was a single informal impression. The remedy is a structured, blind, multi-rater rubric triangulated against two axes independent of human impression, and a claim is strong only when the three agree. \Cref{tab:generator-evaluation-axes} gives the axes and what was carried out, since that gap shapes how the results read.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{The three evaluation axes as designed, and their execution status.}
\label{tab:generator-evaluation-axes}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lXl@{}}
\toprule
Axis & Instrument & Executed \\
\midrule
A: Qualitative & Blind multi-rater rubric with inter-rater agreement & No \\
B: Automatic proxies & Teacher recognition, FID/KID, CLIP-score, yield & Yield only \\
C: Downstream utility & Detection accuracy on real photographs & Yes, all $12$ cells \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Axis A: Structured Qualitative Rubric}
Six criteria were pre-registered: anatomical correctness, diagnostic-feature fidelity, species identity, habitat plausibility, photorealism, artifact rate. The protocol around them is what would make the axis thesis-grade rather than impressionistic: hidden model identity, randomized order, multiple raters and a frozen rubric. It is also the only instrument that could separate generators on the rare classes, where \Cref{sec:results_per_class_structure} shows accuracy at a noise floor. It was never executed.

\paragraph{Axis B: Automatic Proxies}
Four measures were specified, all scaling to every image: teacher-recognition confidence from the project's own classifier \cite{gadotCropNotCrop2024a}, per-class Fréchet and kernel distances against real images of the class \cite{heuselGANsTrainedTwo2018,binkowskiDemystifyingMMDGANs2021}, a CLIP-based image-prompt alignment score \cite{hesselCLIPScoreReferencefreeEvaluation2021}, and the annotation yield. Only the yield was computed, which is why the results chapter leads with downstream accuracy rather than a converged judgment.

\paragraph{Axis C: Downstream Utility}
The fixed detector trains on each cell's synthetic images and is evaluated on real photographs only (\Cref{sec:evaluation_framework}). The headline measure is mean average precision over the real test set. Per-class average precision separates the data-rich anchors from the rare classes. The within-group confusion rate over the three zebra species, the fraction of correctly localized detections landing in the right look-alike group but the wrong species, is the sharpest available test of whether synthetic data teaches species-level discrimination. Only the zebras have more than one member of a frozen look-alike group (\Cref{sec:lookalike_groups}) here, so the bear group's rate is structurally zero and excluded.

\paragraph{Decision Rule}
The criterion was set in advance. A generator counts as usable if it lands within a stated margin of the production generator on the axes that matter, downstream accuracy on real photographs and diagnostic fidelity, \emph{and} is practical on cost and throughput. Raw photorealism alone is insufficient because it is the impression that motivated the original choice. The production choice is justified only if that generator is at or near the top. Since differences in a dataset this small may fall within run-to-run noise, the results are reported as a mean with its spread across seeds. Two cells whose intervals overlap are indistinguishable rather than ranked.
