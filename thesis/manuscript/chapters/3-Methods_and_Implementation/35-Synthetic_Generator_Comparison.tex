\section{Comparing Synthetic-Image Generators}\label{sec:generator_comparison}

The synthetic imagery of \Cref{sec:synthetic_data_supplementation} came from a single generative model, chosen early on a visual comparison against a few alternatives. That choice is load-bearing. If a cheaper or free model would have served as well, or if apparent realism does not translate into detection accuracy, the conclusions of the synthetic-data experiments change. The second possibility is not hypothetical. Earlier work by the present author found that generated image context conferred no downstream advantage over random noise on a small-object car-detection task \cite{hedrichGeneratingSyntheticDatasets2026}. This section describes the controlled comparison built to test it: what was compared, how it was made fair, and how the generators were judged.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Motivation and Scoped Research Question}\label{sec:generator_comparison_motivation}

The work's question about synthetic data is whether current image-generation models can produce training data useful for wildlife detection, particularly for long-tail species. \enquote{Useful} has meaning only against alternatives, and a single-model study cannot separate a property of synthetic data from a property of one vendor's model. The scoped, secondary question is therefore:

\begin{quote}
On a purposively chosen subset of classes, how do synthetic images from different generators (proprietary API services and locally-run open-weight models) compare, both under a structured qualitative rubric and under downstream detection accuracy measured on real photographs, and does the resulting ranking justify the production choice?
\end{quote}

The production run had already consumed most of the synthetic-image budget, so regenerating the full dataset per candidate was never an option. The comparison runs on a reduced class set at a modest per-class image count.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\subsection{Class-Subset Selection}\label{sec:generator_comparison_classes}

The subset must expose three phenomena: long-tail species under-represented in the generators' pretraining data, classes with enough real test images to separate a difference from noise, and near-identical species that test fine-grained markers. The rarest species also have the fewest real test images, so the subset covers all three as a set rather than in every class.

\Cref{tab:generator-comparison-classes} lists the resulting $12$ classes. The rarity proxy is the per-species GBIF count, which caps near $300$ and is evidence of scarcity only at its low end (\Cref{sec:target_class_definition}).

This does not bias the comparison, since every generator is scored on the identical set, but the set is specific to this sub-study and is not the project's general benchmark.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Prompt Regimes}\label{sec:generator_comparison_prompts}

The production prompts run to approximately $1{,}300$ words, while the SDXL-family text encoder stops reading after $77$ tokens \cite{radfordLearningTransferableVisual2021}. The same text therefore does not give both models the same information, and a gap measured that way confounds how well a model generates with how much it read.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{The three prompt regimes and what each is for.}
\label{tab:generator-prompt-regimes}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}llX@{}}
\toprule
Regime & Budget & Purpose \\
\midrule
\texttt{full} & $\approx 1{,}300$ words & Each API model at its maximum, as in production \\
\texttt{compressed} & $\leq 75$ tokens & An \emph{identical} prompt every model can read \\
\texttt{maxlen} & $75/256/512$ tokens & Each local model at its encoder's capacity \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The \texttt{compressed} prompt is dense and ordered by importance, since an encoder weights early tokens most heavily and discards the rest. A trimming rule drops habitat first, then pose, then reduces diagnostic features to one, so the species name and at least one marker survive longest.

The \texttt{maxlen} regime is built by accumulation rather than truncation, growing a short template until the next description sentence would breach the budget. Per-image pose and environment carry over from the production image list, so the prompts keep real per-image variation. Measured lengths sit under the nominal ceilings (\Cref{sec:appendix_prompt_token_budgets}). \Cref{fig:prompt-regime-example} shows the scale of the difference for one class.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\begin{minipage}{0.95\textwidth}

Two asymmetries are disclosed rather than smoothed over. The \texttt{full} regime is not byte-identical across vendors, since one API caps prompts at $32{,}000$ characters, so species with long articles had their description trimmed. And negative prompts could not be applied uniformly, because the FLUX-family pipeline accepts none, so one cell lacks a control the others have.

Reproducibility differs by tier. The local models are deterministic, with per-image seeds from a fixed class ordering. The API services expose no seed, so there the archived images and request parameters are the reproducible artifact. Within the local tier the two short regimes index seeds differently, so the same slot in each was not generated from the same seed, which adds noise to any comparison between them.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Annotation, Detector and Training Protocol}\label{sec:generator_comparison_training}

Every cell was annotated by the detector \cite{beeryEfficientPipelineCamera2019} and procedure of \Cref{sec:synthetic_annotation}. One deviation qualifies every downstream number in \Cref{sec:results_synthetic_data_practicability}: the human review stages were never executed here, so all cells use the detector's own boxes. The ranking is unlikely to be an annotation artifact, since every cell was annotated identically, but the absolute figures are not final. Single-animal yield ranged from $93.6\%$ to $98.8\%$ across the local cells and is itself a quality signal.

\textit{YOLO26n} \cite{UltralyticsYOLO262025} was fixed as the detector for every cell. The available alternative, \textit{YOLOv5s} \cite{ultralyticsComprehensiveGuideUltralytics}, is roughly the size of the detector that already ships, whereas \textit{YOLO26n} is the architecture class this work targets for embedded deployment, so a result on it stays load-bearing for the main study.

Model selection and early stopping use a per-class stratified fifth of each cell's synthetic images, drawn with a seed independent of the training seed, so the split is identical across a cell's repeated runs. The real test set is scored once per run, on the best checkpoint.

Because a single run on a dataset this small is noisy, each cell was trained with several seeds and averaged: three per API cell, two per local cell, short of the three the protocol recommends.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Evaluation Methodology}\label{sec:generator_comparison_evaluation}

The judgment this sub-study replaces was a single informal impression. The remedy is a structured, blind, multi-rater rubric triangulated against two axes independent of human impression, and a claim is strong only when the three agree. \Cref{tab:generator-evaluation-axes} gives the axes and what was carried out, since that gap shapes how the results read.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{The three evaluation axes as designed, and their execution status.}
\label{tab:generator-evaluation-axes}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lXl@{}}
\toprule
Axis & Instrument & Executed \\
\midrule
A: Qualitative & Blind multi-rater rubric with inter-rater agreement & No \\
B: Automatic proxies & Teacher recognition, FID/KID, CLIP-score, yield & Yield only \\
C: Downstream utility & Detection accuracy on real photographs & Yes, all $12$ cells \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Axis A: Structured Qualitative Rubric}
Six criteria were pre-registered: anatomical correctness, diagnostic-feature fidelity, species identity, habitat plausibility, photorealism, artifact rate. The protocol around them is what would make the axis thesis-grade rather than impressionistic: hidden model identity, randomised order, multiple raters and a frozen rubric. It is also the only instrument that could separate generators on the rare classes, where \Cref{sec:results_per_class_structure} shows accuracy at a noise floor. It was never executed.

\paragraph{Axis B: Automatic Proxies}
Four measures were specified, all scaling to every image: teacher-recognition confidence from the project's own classifier \cite{gadotCropNotCrop2024a}, per-class Fréchet and kernel distances against real images of the class \cite{heuselGANsTrainedTwo2018,binkowskiDemystifyingMMDGANs2021}, a CLIP-based image-prompt alignment score \cite{hesselCLIPScoreReferencefreeEvaluation2021}, and the annotation yield. Only the yield was computed, which is why the results chapter leads with downstream accuracy rather than a converged judgment.

\paragraph{Axis C: Downstream Utility}
The fixed detector trains on each cell's synthetic images and is evaluated on real photographs only (\Cref{sec:evaluation_framework}). The headline measure is mean average precision over the real test set. Per-class average precision separates the data-rich anchors from the rare classes. The within-group confusion rate over the three zebra species, the fraction of correctly localised detections landing in the right look-alike group but the wrong species, is the sharpest available test of whether synthetic data teaches species-level discrimination. Only the zebras have more than one member of a frozen look-alike group (\Cref{sec:gt_annotation_integrity}) here, so the bear group's rate is structurally zero and excluded.

\paragraph{Decision Rule}
The criterion was fixed in advance. A generator counts as usable if it lands within a stated margin of the incumbent on the axes that matter, downstream accuracy on real photographs and diagnostic fidelity, \emph{and} is practical on cost and throughput. Raw photorealism alone is not sufficient, since it is the impression that motivated the original choice. The production choice is justified only if the incumbent sits at or near the top. Because differences on a dataset this small may fall within run-to-run noise, results are reported as a mean with its spread across seeds, and two cells whose intervals overlap are indistinguishable rather than ranked.
