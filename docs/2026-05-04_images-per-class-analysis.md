# **Optimising Edge-Deployed Wildlife Detection Models: A Comparative Analysis of Knowledge Distillation and Direct Fine-Tuning Across Data Scarcity Brackets**

## **1\. Executive Context and Architectural Paradigm**

The deployment of deep learning-based computer vision models for remote ecological monitoring introduces a severe intersection of operational constraints: extreme hardware limitations, highly imbalanced long-tail datasets, and the necessity for real-time inference in unstructured environments.1 The objective of detecting 225 non-bird mammal species on a Qualcomm QCS605 System-on-Chip (SoC)—leveraging its Hexagon 685 Digital Signal Processor (DSP) and Adreno 615 Graphics Processing Unit (GPU)—requires navigating the delicate trade-off between model capacity and generalisation capability. While a Raspberry Pi 5 equipped with 8 GB of RAM serves as a capable proxy during the initial development phases, the ultimate QCS605 target necessitates the stringent use of nano-scale convolutional architectures. These architectures, such as YOLOv11n, YOLOv12n, NanoDet-Plus-m, and PicoDet-S, are specifically designed to contain fewer than 3 million parameters, making them suitable for the stringent memory and thermal envelopes of edge computing.3

The core architectural investigation of this research contrasts two distinct training paradigms. Track A utilises Knowledge Distillation (KD), a technique wherein a larger, highly capable "teacher" model is first fine-tuned on the wildlife dataset. Its intermediate feature representations and output probability distributions are subsequently distilled into the compact "student" model prior to Quantisation-Aware Training (QAT).6 For this track, the teacher candidates include SpeciesNet, which employs an EfficientNetV2-M backbone (\~54 M parameters) pre-trained on a massive global biodiversity dataset, and YOLOv8s (\~11 M parameters), a mid-scale object detector.8 Conversely, Track B establishes an empirical baseline by directly fine-tuning the nano-student models on the sparse dataset, bypassing the teacher entirely, followed by identical QAT and deployment steps.11

The dataset itself introduces significant volatility and complexity to the training pipeline. An initial raw repository of 457,279 images suffers an approximate 65.9% attrition rate during rigorous quality filtering. This pipeline, which sequentially applies metadata checks, MegaDetector bounding box generation, and SpeciesNet classification confidence thresholds (≥ 0.3), reduces the viable pool of imagery to approximately 158,667 images.10 This dramatic reduction fundamentally alters the training landscape. Classes previously deemed "Excellent" based on raw counts are abruptly shifted into marginal or critical scarcity brackets. The subsequent analysis comprehensively dissects the specific behavioural mechanics, feature extraction capabilities, and quantisation viability of these neural architectures across predefined data scarcity brackets, culminating in targeted strategic recommendations for the training pipeline.

## **2\. Theoretical Foundations of the Model Architectures and Distillation Mechanics**

Understanding the behavior of these models across varying gradients of data scarcity requires a foundational analysis of their architectural differences, as well as the mathematical mechanisms that drive Knowledge Distillation in the context of object detection.

### **2.1 Nano-Scale Student Architectures**

The student models under consideration represent the state-of-the-art in efficient, anchor-free object detection. PicoDet-S, at a mere 0.99 M parameters, utilises an Enhanced ShuffleNet (ESNet) backbone and a Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) to extract features with minimal computational overhead.5 NanoDet-Plus-m (1.17 M parameters) employs a Ghost-PAN feature pyramid and an Assign Guidance Module (AGM) alongside a Dynamic Soft Label Assigner (DSLA) to solve optimal label assignment problems during lightweight model training.4 The YOLO series variants (YOLOv11n and YOLOv12n, at \~2.6 M parameters) leverage advanced Distribution Focal Loss (DFL) and highly optimized bottleneck blocks to push the Pareto frontier of accuracy and latency.3

Despite their architectural efficiency, these models possess a critical vulnerability: their shallow feature hierarchies drastically limit their representational capacity. When exposed to high intra-class variance—such as a mammal appearing in different lighting, postures, or degrees of occlusion—these nano-models struggle to form a cohesive latent representation without a dense manifold of training examples.18

### **2.2 Mid-Scale Teacher Architectures**

The teacher models possess significantly deeper feature hierarchies. YOLOv8s (\~11 M parameters) provides a robust anchor-free detection framework capable of extracting multi-scale semantic information from complex backgrounds.3 SpeciesNet is built upon the EfficientNetV2-M backbone (\~54 M parameters), which utilizes compound scaling of depth, width, and resolution to maximize accuracy.9 Crucially, SpeciesNet has been pre-trained on a dataset of over 65 million images contributed by global conservation organizations, granting it an unparalleled prior understanding of morphological features across over 2,000 taxonomic categories.8 This extensive pre-training acts as a massive regularization prior, allowing it to interpret scarce data without immediately overfitting to background noise.

### **2.3 Knowledge Distillation in Object Detection**

Knowledge Distillation (KD) transfers "dark knowledge" from the teacher to the student. In standard classification, this involves training the student to mimic the "soft targets" (probability distributions) generated by the teacher, calculated via Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence with a temperature scaling parameter (![][image1]) to soften the logits.6

However, applying KD to object detection introduces unique challenges. Detection tasks inherently suffer from an extreme foreground-background class imbalance; the vast majority of bounding box anchors correspond to background pixels.6 If a student model blindly distils all outputs from a teacher, it overwhelmingly distils background noise, degrading performance. Advanced object detection KD techniques, therefore, rely on feature imitation—forcing the student's Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) to mimic the intermediate feature maps of the teacher—and decoupled distillation, which isolates objectness masks to ensure the student only learns from regions of interest.6 The efficacy of this distillation is fundamentally dependent on the reliability of the teacher's predictions; if the teacher is compromised by data scarcity, the student inherits its hallucinations.26

## **3\. Analysis of Data Scarcity Brackets**

The volume of per-class training imagery fundamentally dictates the convergence stability, feature representation, and quantisation viability of both the student and teacher architectures. The following analysis evaluates the practical effects of specific image count brackets on the Track A (KD) and Track B (Direct Fine-Tuning) pipelines.

### **3.1 The Extreme Few-Shot Regime: 10 Images per Class**

**Nano-Scale Students (0.99 M – 2.6 M params):** At 10 images per class, nano-architectures such as PicoDet-S, NanoDet-Plus-m, and YOLOv11n undergo immediate and catastrophic overfitting.28 These models completely lack the regularisation capacity necessary to extract generalised spatial hierarchies from such a minuscule sample size. In Track B, the student models will rapidly memorize the specific background pixels and artifacts present in the 10 images rather than the morphological features of the mammal.2 Consequently, they will exhibit a near 100% false negative rate when deployed in unseen environments, failing to generalize to any novel posture or lighting condition. Knowledge distillation from Track A cannot meaningfully compensate for data this sparse; the student lacks the capacity to project the teacher's complex, high-dimensional feature space into its shallow parameters based on only 10 spatial examples.

**Mid-Scale Teachers (11 M – 54 M params):** While pre-trained foundational models like SpeciesNet possess deep latent representations of global biodiversity, attempting to fine-tune an 11 M or 54 M parameter model on 10 images actively degrades its pre-existing weights without establishing a new, reliable boundary for the target domain.30 The teacher model fails to adapt to the specific camera trap domain shift and will default erratically to its pre-training biases or overfit to the training batch.32

**Class Imbalance Effects:** In a dataset exhibiting a severe long-tail distribution, training a class with 10 images alongside classes possessing 5,000 to 30,000 images results in total gradient subsumption.33 The gradients generated by the majority classes completely overwhelm the optimization trajectory. The network essentially learns a prior probability that the minority class does not exist. The model will consistently hallucinate the majority class when confronted with the minority class.

**Impact on KD Effectiveness:** At 10 images, the soft labels produced by the teacher are highly erratic and uncalibrated.26 Distilling these confident hallucinations actively damages the student's representation.35 The teacher acts as a source of noise rather than a source of truth, rendering Track A highly detrimental at this bracket.

**Domain Shift Consideration:** Wildlife photography is characterised by high intra-class variance due to unpredictable natural environments.2 Ten images cannot capture the diurnal lighting shifts, weather variations, or partial occlusions native to camera traps. The domain shift completely invalidates any learning achieved at this bracket.

### **3.2 The Marginal Transfer Regime: 30 Images per Class**

**Nano-Scale Students:** At 30 images, models utilising advanced anchor-free mechanisms (such as NanoDet-Plus-m with its Generalized Focal Loss and YOLOv11n with Distribution Focal Loss) begin to approximate rudimentary objectness, successfully placing loose bounding boxes around foreground anomalies.4 However, species-level classification accuracy remains practically unusable.19 The models can detect that an animal is present but fail to differentiate between morphologically similar species (e.g., distinguishing a coyote from a wolf). Overfitting risk remains at a critical level.

**Mid-Scale Teachers:** A YOLOv8s teacher may begin to recognise highly distinctive, uniformly presented species (e.g., zebras with clear striping), but consistently fails on species with high intra-class variance or camouflaging traits. The feature maps extracted from the mid-scale teacher are brittle, heavily relying on spurious correlations such as specific foliage patterns or camera angles present in the 30 images.2

**Class Imbalance Effects:**

The minority class continues to be severely suppressed by head classes. In Track B, the student will almost entirely ignore the 30-image class to optimize the cross-entropy loss on the 30,000-image classes. In Track A, the teacher begins to offer slight resistance against this suppression, but it is insufficient to correct the fundamental imbalance.

**Impact on KD Effectiveness:** KD at 30 images introduces the transfer of "dark knowledge," but the quality of this knowledge is heavily compromised.6 While the teacher might output a softer probability distribution (e.g., 0.4 Cheetah, 0.3 Leopard, 0.3 Background), the calibration of these probabilities is statistically invalid at this sample size.26 Distillation at this stage acts merely as a regularisation penalty against network overconfidence, rather than a transfer of true morphological understanding.

**Domain Shift Consideration:** Even if 30 images were sufficient for a highly controlled environment (e.g., industrial part inspection), they are entirely inadequate for the wilderness. The minimum viable count for camera traps is structurally higher due to the inability to control the subject's distance, posture, or interaction with the environment.38

### **3.3 The Distillation Divergence Threshold: 50 Images per Class**

**Nano-Scale Students:** Fifty images represent a crucial transitional threshold. In Track B, direct fine-tuning still yields unacceptably high false-positive rates and poor recall, but aggressive data augmentation techniques can artificially inflate the sample variance just enough to prevent total background memorisation.19 The student begins to form rudimentary, albeit unstable, clusters in its latent space.

**Mid-Scale Teachers:** At 50 images, a mid-scale teacher with a frozen backbone and a carefully fine-tuned classification head begins to stabilize.40 SpeciesNet, empowered by its 65 million image pre-training, can leverage its massive prior knowledge to extract meaningful semantic features from 50 high-quality, filtered samples.8 It successfully maps the new species into its pre-existing taxonomy.

**Class Imbalance Effects:** This is the bracket where Track A and Track B begin to exhibit divergent behaviors regarding class imbalance. In Track B, the student still succumbs to the majority class bias. In Track A, however, the teacher model's calibrated logits provide a continuous, non-zero gradient for the minority classes during every forward pass, even when evaluating majority class images.41 This prevents the minority class weights from collapsing entirely.

**Impact on KD Effectiveness:** The teacher model can now produce marginally reliable soft targets and robust intermediate feature maps.42 Instead of the nano-student receiving a rigid, potentially noisy one-hot vector (Track B), it receives a probability distribution that accurately reflects morphological similarities between closely related mammal families.43 This soft supervision prevents the student from forcing noisy gradients into its shallow feature pyramid network, allowing Track A to yield measurable mean Average Precision (mAP) improvements over Track B.11

**Domain Shift Consideration:** Fifty images, if perfectly curated to cover varied lighting and angles, can begin to bridge the domain gap. However, because these 50 images are drawn from a larger pool via a 65.9% failure-rate filter, they are likely to be biased toward ideal daytime conditions, leaving the model blind to nocturnal or heavily occluded scenarios.10

### **3.4 The Foundational Baseline Regime: 100 Images per Class**

**Nano-Scale Students:** One hundred images per class is universally recognised across transfer-learning literature as the absolute minimum baseline threshold for fine-tuning modern object detection models like YOLOv11n and PicoDet.15 At this bracket, the student possesses enough geometric data to form distinct cluster representations. However, generalisation remains strictly bounded by the diversity of the 100 images. If the images lack varied camera angles, the model will fail to recognize the animal from the side or rear.46

**Mid-Scale Teachers:** YOLOv8s and EfficientNetV2-M reach a functional, dependable state of domain adaptation at 100 images.40 The teacher models can consistently extract robust feature maps and identify species despite moderate domain shifts, reliably separating foreground mammals from complex backgrounds.

**Class Imbalance Effects:** When trained alongside a 30,000-image class, a 100-image class still suffers from severe long-tail gradient suppression.49 Track B models will exhibit highly skewed precision-recall curves, favoring recall on the head classes and precision on the tail classes (i.e., only predicting the tail class when absolutely certain, missing many true positives).50 Track A mitigates this, utilizing feature distillation to enforce representational equality in the intermediate layers.6

**Impact on KD Effectiveness:** The teacher's predictions at 100 images become statistically reliable enough to act as a firm anchor for the student.37 Feature-based distillation becomes highly effective here, as the teacher can reliably guide the student's spatial attention toward the correct anatomical features of the animal, rather than the surrounding foliage.23

**Domain Shift Consideration:** While 100 images might suffice for standard COCO-style benchmarks, the intra-class variance of wildlife (seasonal fur changes, diverse postures, erratic distances) means 100 images only covers a fraction of potential real-world states.2 The minimum viable count for ecological monitoring remains structurally higher than that for industrial or urban computer vision tasks.

### **3.5 The Developing Stability Regime: 200 Images per Class**

**Nano-Scale Students:** At 200 images, the risk of catastrophic overfitting diminishes significantly.28 The integration of advanced augmentation pipelines—such as Mosaic and MixUp—allows the 2.6 M parameters of YOLOv11n to learn translation and scale invariance efficiently.53 The model begins to reliably track moving animals across different frames of a camera trap sequence.

**Mid-Scale Teachers:** Teacher models operate with high efficacy, successfully navigating complex scenes containing multiple animals or severe partial occlusions.55

**Class Imbalance Effects:** The 200 instances provide enough geometric loss signals to prevent the class from collapsing under the weight of the majority classes. In Track A, the teacher model's ability to retain feature relationships protects the student from ignoring the minority class.41 The distillation loss acts as an effective mathematical buffer against the overwhelming background and majority-class gradients.23

**Impact on KD Effectiveness:** KD provides a highly stable training trajectory. The soft targets are well-calibrated, reflecting true semantic uncertainty rather than epistemic noise due to lack of data.26

**Domain Shift Consideration:** At 200 images, the dataset begins to naturally encompass diurnal and nocturnal shifts, assuming the data collection was temporally unbiased. The model's robustness to varying camera trap models and sensor qualities improves markedly.38

### **3.6 The Ecological Viability Threshold: 500 Images per Class**

**Nano-Scale Students:** A bracket of 500 images per class is the point at which nano-models achieve true ecological viability for edge deployment.57 The student models possess sufficient empirical examples to map the distinct texture, shape, and context of the mammals. The models become highly resistant to hallucinating animals in empty vegetation—a pervasive issue in camera trap deployments.13

**Mid-Scale Teachers:** The teacher model operates with definitive confidence. Its feature maps are highly discriminative, easily separating morphologically similar species.26

**Class Imbalance Effects:** At 500 images, the minority classes have sufficient batch representation to generate consistent, meaningful gradient updates.34 While re-weighting techniques (such as focal loss) are still necessary, the fundamental architecture is no longer starved for data.56

**Impact on KD Effectiveness:** Distillation at this bracket shifts from merely "preventing overfitting" to actively "transferring complex representational logic".37 The student learns the precise boundary conditions of bounding boxes from the teacher, vastly improving Intersection over Union (IoU) metrics.56

**Domain Shift & Augmentation Impact:** At 500 images, sophisticated "copy-paste" augmentation becomes extraordinarily effective.60 By segmenting the mammals from the 500 diverse source images and pasting them onto entirely novel, empty ecological backgrounds, the effective dataset size scales multiplicatively. Empirical studies demonstrate that this technique yields an observed 8% ± 2% increase in mAP, effectively simulating the variance of a 1,000+ image dataset and neutralizing domain shift.57

### **3.7 The Maturation Range: 1,000 Images per Class**

**Nano-Scale Students:** At 1,000 images per class, both Track A and Track B yield highly performant, robust models.45 The nano-students have ample data to fully saturate their limited parameter counts (0.99 M to 2.6 M).45

**Impact on KD Effectiveness:** Interestingly, the performance delta between Track A (KD) and Track B (Direct Fine-Tuning) begins to narrow significantly at this bracket.62 When the student has 1,000 ground-truth examples, the rigid one-hot labels provide sufficient, high-quality gradients for optimal convergence.42 While Knowledge Distillation still provides marginal gains in bounding box regression (via feature imitation) and edge-case classification, it is no longer the sole mechanism preventing model collapse.11

**Domain Shift Consideration:** Domain shift is largely mitigated. Because 1,000 quality-filtered images encompass the vast majority of natural poses, lighting conditions (dawn, dusk, mid-day, infrared), and weather variations expected in the field, the model can safely interpolate between known states rather than extrapolating blindly.38

### **3.8 The Saturation Target: ≥ 1,500 Images per Class**

**Model Dynamics and Ultimate Performance:** This bracket represents the Ultralytics-recommended gold standard for stable, commercial-grade object detection.45 At ≥ 1,500 quality-filtered images, models like YOLOv11n and NanoDet-Plus-m reach their theoretical maximum accuracy for their given architectural capacity.4 Providing additional data beyond this point yields diminishing returns unless the architectural capacity (parameter count) is simultaneously increased.

**Class Imbalance Normalisation:** A class with 1,500 images, even when trained alongside a peak class with 30,000 images, possesses enough critical mass to command equal representational space in the network's latent geometry, provided standard focal loss or class re-weighting techniques are actively applied.34

**Hardware Deployment:** Models trained at this bracket and subsequently deployed on the QCS605's Hexagon DSP will exhibit maximal recall, minimal latency, and robust resistance to false positives.19 The activation statistics gathered during training will be perfectly representative of the deployment environment, ensuring flawless INT8 quantisation.

**4\. Hardware Constraints: Quantisation-Aware Training on the QCS605**

The ultimate deployment target, the Qualcomm QCS605 System-on-Chip, heavily utilises the Hexagon 685 DSP to accelerate neural network inference. This acceleration is achieved through 8-bit integer (INT8) quantisation, reducing the memory footprint and computational latency compared to standard 32-bit floating-point (FP32) execution.64 This strict hardware reality imposes severe, non-negotiable constraints on the minimum required data volumes.

Quantisation-Aware Training (QAT) simulates the effects of reduced numerical precision during the training's forward pass. It utilizes a standard quantizer function: ![][image2], which transforms continuous, high-precision values into discrete, lower-precision representations.65 By introducing these quantisation effects during training, QAT enables the model to optimise its parameters with an awareness of the reduced precision, adjusting its weights to minimize the inevitable quantisation error.65

Crucially, to execute this accurately, QAT relies on activation statistics—specifically, moving averages of minimum and maximum activation values across the training batches—to determine the optimal clipping ranges (![][image3]) and scaling factors (![][image4]).67

**The Low-Data Quantisation Penalty:** If a class contains only 10, 30, or even 50 images, the activation distributions observed during the QAT process will be highly skewed, statistically sparse, and fundamentally unrepresentative of the real-world operational domain.67 The QAT algorithm will establish rigid clipping thresholds based on this artificially constrained data.

When the model is subsequently compiled, deployed on the Hexagon DSP, and exposed to novel lighting or angles in the wild, the activations generated by these novel inputs will fall far outside the narrowly calibrated range. This results in catastrophic clipping errors, where massive amounts of valid feature data are truncated to the maximum or minimum INT8 value, destroying the feature map's integrity.64 Therefore, while 50 images might be theoretically viable for an FP32 model running on an Adreno GPU, **effective INT8 Quantisation-Aware Training demands a significantly higher volume of diverse data simply to establish statistically sound, robust activation ranges for DSP deployment.**

**5\. The Architecture of Class Imbalance and Long-Tail Dynamics**

The dataset under review exhibits an extreme long-tail distribution: peak classes possess \> 30,000 images, while the tail degrades to \< 30\. In object detection, this class-level imbalance is severely compounded by a spatial imbalance: the overwhelming ratio of foreground object pixels to empty background pixels.23

### **5.1 Gradient Suppression in Track B (Direct Fine-Tuning)**

In Track B, directly fine-tuning a shallow 2.6 M parameter model on a long-tail dataset forces the stochastic gradient descent (SGD) or AdamW optimiser to prioritise the head classes to minimise the overall global loss.49 During random batch sampling, a minority class (e.g., a rare primate with 50 images) will rarely appear. When it does, its gradients are immediately overwhelmed by the accumulated momentum of the head classes (e.g., deer with 30,000 images).

In standard cross-entropy loss, the network learns to push the logits of the incorrect classes down. Because the head classes appear constantly, the network continually pushes the logits of the rare classes toward zero. Consequently, the model establishes rigid decision boundaries that heavily favour the head classes, leading to the confident misclassification of rare mammals as common ones.32

### **5.2 The Protective Mechanics of Track A (Knowledge Distillation)**

Knowledge Distillation offers a profound structural advantage in long-tail scenarios, acting as a dynamic mathematical regularizer.41 A robust mid-scale teacher, particularly one pre-trained on a balanced global taxonomy like SpeciesNet, maintains a well-calibrated, distinct latent space for even the rarest species.35

When the teacher evaluates an image of a majority class, its soft label output is not a 100% rigid probability. It assigns fractional, mathematically logical probabilities to visually similar minority classes.37 For example, when viewing a common white-tailed deer, the teacher might output a 0.05 probability for a rare antelope.

When the student model trains on these soft labels, it continuously updates the weights associated with the minority classes, even when it is physically processing images of the majority class.6 This constant, low-amplitude gradient flow provides persistent representation for the tail classes, preventing their weights from collapsing to zero. Thus, Track A is substantially more resilient to the 30,000-vs-30 image disparity than the direct approach of Track B.34

**6\. Strategic Pipeline Recommendations**

Based on the synthesis of architectural constraints, long-tail dynamics, the mechanics of Knowledge Distillation, and the strict numerical demands of the QCS605's Hexagon DSP, the following concrete recommendations are provided to define the optimal training strategy for the Master's thesis.

### **6.1 Hard Minimum Threshold (Exclusion Criteria)**

**Recommendation: Exclude any class with fewer than 100 post-filter images from both Track A and Track B.**

*Justification:* Empirical evidence strictly dictates that below 100 images, anchor-free bounding-box regression fails on nano-scale models, leading to severe localisation errors.15 Furthermore, the QCS605 DSP requires robust activation statistics for effective INT8 Quantisation-Aware Training.65 Fewer than 100 images will result in statistically fragile calibration ranges, causing the DSP to aggressively clip valid activations during inference, destroying accuracy. In Track A, the teacher's soft labels below 100 images contain unmanageable epistemic uncertainty; distilling this uncertainty means the student will merely memorize the teacher's hallucinations.26 Excluding these critically sparse classes entirely prevents the gradient poisoning of the viable classes.

### **6.2 Soft Target Thresholds**

The "soft target" represents the ideal minimum per-class count required to guarantee acceptable, ecologically viable detection quality without inducing severe overfitting or generalisation failure.

**Track A (Teacher Fine-Tuning): 500 post-filter images** *Justification:* Mid-scale models like SpeciesNet (54 M params) or YOLOv8s (11 M params) possess massive, complex feature spaces. To overcome the specific domain shift of local camera traps and re-align their classification heads without experiencing catastrophic forgetting of their pre-trained weights, a minimum of 500 diverse instances is required to establish a robust, generalisable decision boundary.40

**Track A (Student Trained via KD): 300 post-filter images** *Justification:* Because the student receives rich, continuous probability distributions (soft labels) and feature-map guidance from the teacher, it extracts significantly more usable information per image than in a standard hard-label setup.37 The strong regularising effect of KD allows the nano-student (e.g., YOLOv11n) to reach convergence stability at 300 images, heavily relying on the teacher's transferred "dark knowledge" to bridge the geometric gaps in its understanding.6

**Track B (Direct Student Fine-Tuning): 1,000 post-filter images** *Justification:* Without the architectural guidance of a teacher model, the nano-student is highly vulnerable to background memorisation and extreme long-tail gradient suppression.34 To autonomously learn translation-invariant features, handle occlusion, and build robust bounding box anchors using only rigid one-hot labels, the student requires a dense, comprehensive empirical manifold. This necessitates a strict minimum of 1,000 images to succeed on embedded hardware.45

### **6.3 Tiered Strategy: Class Set Reduction**

**Recommendation: Reduce the target class set from the original 225 down to approximately 100–120 classes.**

*Justification:* The provided dataset metrics indicate that currently, 83 classes possess ≥ 1,500 *raw* images, and 24 possess 1,000–1,499. Given that the rigorous quality filtering pipeline eliminates approximately 66% of images, the 83 "Excellent" classes will reduce to roughly 500 usable images. This perfectly aligns with the Track A teacher soft target of 500 images. However, the 88 classes that currently possess \< 500 raw images will be utterly decimated to \< 160 usable images post-filtering. Retaining these highly sparse classes will destabilise the loss function, introduce extreme long-tail bias, and cripple the INT8 QAT process.35 By aggressively trimming the dataset to only include classes possessing a minimum of 300 raw images (yielding \~100 post-filter images), the research can definitively isolate the architectural performance delta between Track A and Track B without the noise of extreme data starvation confounding the empirical results.11

### **6.4 Post-Filter Counts vs. Raw Counts (The Multiplier Effect)**

**Recommendation: Plan data collection, class inclusion, and scarcity thresholds strictly based on post-filter counts, assuming a 3.0x multiplier requirement from raw data.**

*Justification:* The initial heuristic assumption of a 20% quality buffer is fatally optimistic and statistically inaccurate. The empirical data demonstrates a massive 65.9% failure rate during the SpeciesNet filtering phase.10 This high attrition rate is standard for remote ecological datasets; images are correctly rejected because animals are too occluded, severely motion-blurred, or fall below the strict confidence threshold required to ensure training label integrity.13 Therefore, the effective usable dataset is roughly 34% of the raw intake. To achieve the Track A student target of 300 usable images, the pipeline must ingest **900 raw images**. To reach the Track B target of 1,000 usable images, the pipeline requires **3,000 raw images**. All thresholds must be rigidly defined by the post-filter counts, as these are the exact, verified tensors passing through the backpropagation algorithm.

### **6.5 The Role of Augmentation in Data-Sparse Classes**

**Recommendation: Implement advanced Copy-Paste and Mosaic augmentations to artificially elevate data-sparse classes, acting as a 2x to 3x effective data variance multiplier.**

*Justification:* Standard, rudimentary augmentations (colour jitter, random cropping) do not inject new geometric or semantic information into the network. However, complex spatial augmentations like Mosaic and Copy-Paste fundamentally alter the learning paradigm, effectively expanding the dataset.53

* **Copy-Paste Augmentation:** By extracting mammal instances using precise segmentation masks and pasting them onto diverse, empty camera-trap backgrounds (which are abundantly available), the model is prevented from associating specific species with specific background foliage.60 Literature demonstrates that Copy-Paste augmentation in wildlife detection improves generalisation and transferability by an average of 8% ± 2% mAP.57 For a class with 200 post-filter images, Copy-Paste can simulate the environmental variance of 600 images, effectively dragging it across the ecological viability threshold.  
* **Mosaic Augmentation:** Splicing four separate images into a single training grid forces the nano-student to detect objects at smaller, non-standard scales and in unpredictable contexts, vastly enhancing the robustness of the Feature Pyramid Network (FPN).72

*Crucial Caveat on Thresholds:* While aggressive augmentation bridges the performance gap and increases variance, it **does not alter the hard minimum threshold of 100 real images**. Augmenting 10 images 50 times merely results in a model highly overfitted to those 10 specific topological poses.57 Augmentation acts as a multiplier of variance, not a substitute for baseline anatomical diversity.

### **6.6 Concluding Strategic Summary**

To answer the core research question effectively and deploy a highly performant model onto the Qualcomm QCS605, the thesis should execute Track A and Track B on a curated, reduced subset of \~120 mammalian species that successfully survive the 3.0x raw-to-post-filter attrition rate.

The analysis dictates that Track A (Knowledge Distillation) will unequivocally outperform Track B (Direct Fine-Tuning) in the 100–500 post-filter image range. This superiority occurs primarily because KD acts as a protective mathematical buffer against severe long-tail gradient suppression. Furthermore, the teacher provides rich, soft-label gradients and highly accurate feature maps that allow the shallow 2.6 M parameters of YOLOv11n or PicoDet-S to converge efficiently, generating robust activation statistics necessary for flawless INT8 quantisation on the Hexagon DSP. Implementing these strict thresholds and augmentation strategies will ensure the deployment of an ecologically viable, real-time edge detection system.
